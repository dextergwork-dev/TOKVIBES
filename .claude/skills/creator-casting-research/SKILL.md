# Creator Casting Research (website only)

## Goal
Recruit the right UGC creators for a brand when all we have is its website. You can't cast well until you understand the product, the real cause of the customer's problem, the new solution, and who actually buys. This skill builds that foundation from public evidence, in the same layout as our Ads Strategy Dashboards, then turns it into a casting brief and outreach the team can use the same day.

## Input
A brand website URL. If the user also shares anything else (client onboarding answers, ad account data, reviews CSV, past sprint learnings), use it and mark those insights as verified.

## Step 1: Research (cite a link for everything)
Do the research yourself before writing anything. Use web search and page fetching.
- **Website:** product pages, About, FAQ, pricing, offer, how it's sold, and any on-site reviews.
- **Reviews:** App Store / Google Play, Trustpilot, Amazon, or wherever this product is reviewed.
- **Reddit and forums:** search the problem it solves, the product category, and the brand name. Find at least 3 relevant threads.
- **Meta Ad Library:** the brand and its top 3 competitors. Note the longest-running ads and which concepts get iterated. Those are the likely winners.
- **Competitors:** identify the top 3 from search results, Reddit mentions and the Ad Library.

If a source can't be reached, say so in the output instead of skipping it silently.

## Step 2: Output these sections, in this order

Start with a **one-glance summary**: who to cast, the core mechanism in one line, and the top 3 intensification lines.

**1. Product snapshot**
What it is and how it works, step by step as the customer experiences it. Price, offer, how it's sold (checkout, app download or lead form), main market, and the top 3 competitors with how this brand is different from each.

**2. Unique mechanisms**
- **UMP (the real cause):** why what they've already tried hasn't worked. The missing 1%.
- **UMS (the real solution):** the new way that fixes the real cause.
- **Unique mechanism name (for scripts):** a branded name, plus a plain-language version a creator could say naturally on camera.
- **Intensification targets (Schwartz's specificity rule):** never the generic claim ("processed food is bad"). Give 5 to 8 specific, concrete lines using real products, real numbers, real places or real moments, like "the yoghurt you bought because it said high protein scored 12/100". Mark any number you couldn't verify.

**3. Pillars table**
| Pillar | Answer |
Authority figure · UMP · UMS · Unique mechanism (described without the product name) · 3 to 5 features tied to benefits.

**4. Customer voice**
Top 5 before-problems, top 5 triggers to try it, top 5 reasons they love it, and top 5 objections. Each with verbatim quotes and the source link. Highlight one "golden" quote that could be a headline.

**5. Persona map: the 3 desires most likely to convert**
For each persona: the thought in their head (first person), the desire (Life Force 8), awareness level, and why they'd act now.

**6. Creator casting brief** (the most important section)
For each of 3 creator profiles:
- **Who:** age range, gender, life stage, accent or region, setting (their home, kitchen, gym, car, store).
- **The one trait that makes them believable:** e.g. "a real mum who already pays more for organic".
- **What they must be able to show:** product use, a live demo, kids on camera or not, filming in-store, a helper behind the phone.
- **Two concept ideas:** one talking to camera (yapper) and one greenscreen explainer, each tied to a different motivator.
- **Red flags:** who NOT to cast, e.g. polished lifestyle influencers or anyone who doesn't look like the buyer.

**7. Creator outreach**
- A short recruitment post for Backstage or a job board: brand, what they'll film, who we want, pay placeholder [$X], timeline.
- 3 to 5 screening questions that catch the usual blockers before we book anyone: Can you film in a supermarket? Do you have someone to hold the phone? Are you comfortable showing your kids, and how old are they? Can you deliver within 48 hours? Do you have access to the product or app?

**8. Gaps to confirm with the client**
Everything marked ❓: KPI, best-performing ads, review exports, product or app access for creators, and anything you couldn't verify. Phrase these as ready-to-ask onboarding questions.

## Rules
- **Label every insight:** ✅ verified (quote or data with a link), 🟡 inferred (pattern across sources), ❓ assumption (needs the client to confirm).
- **Use the customer's exact words** wherever possible. Their language becomes our scripts.
- **No ad account data:** you can't see spend or results, so never present an ad as a "winner" with numbers. Say "longest-running".
- **Flag compliance risks:** health, income or before/after claims. Use "linked to", never "causes".
- **Never fabricate** a quote, review, statistic or ad. If you can't find it, say so.
- **Writing style:** short, plain and scannable. No em dashes. No AI filler.
- **Deliver in chat.** Don't post, send or publish anything. If the user wants it saved, offer to add it as a page in the client's ClickUp Ads Strategy Dashboard.
